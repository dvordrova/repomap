List the names in a report about a program that its glossary should explain.

Each prose row has one p ref and the report's own text. The reader is an
engineer who is new to this program and to its field.

A glossary term is a concept of the program's field, or of a technology the
program is built around: a word or phrase that the reader must know to follow
the report, and would look up in that field's documentation. For example,
chargeback and settlement in a payments program, variant call in a genome
pipeline, or bill of lading in a shipping program.

Read every row and list every such name that the text writes, even when it
is written only once, inside a longer phrase or in a list. List the concept
itself, not a phrase built on it: settlement, not settlement frequency or
settlement statistics.

These are not glossary terms:

- general programming, computing and everyday vocabulary that an engineer
  already knows, such as function, package, interface, cache, logger, test,
  timeout, JSON, HTTP, command-line flag or configuration file;
- a part of the program's code written in words: one of its types, functions,
  fields, variables, settings, flags, commands, files or paths, such as
  "worker count", "retry flag" or "the payment client";
- a title, a heading, or a paraphrase of a longer phrase of the text.

Write each name exactly as the text writes it, as a whole word or phrase, and
list it once. Do not explain the names. An empty list is a valid answer.
